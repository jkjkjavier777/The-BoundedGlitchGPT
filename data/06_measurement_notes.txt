BoundedGlitchEngine — Measurement Notes

Measuring a bounded system honestly is harder than measuring an
unbounded one, because the very thing that makes it interesting —
controlled irregularity — is also the thing most measurement tools
are built to treat as error and discard.

The first rule of measurement here is to never average away the
glitch. A naive metric that reports only a rolling mean will smooth
out exactly the signal that matters most. Instead, dispersion
measures — variance, interquartile range, max deviation per window —
should be reported alongside any central tendency statistic, and
neither should be presented without the other.

Second, measurement windows need to be chosen relative to the cycle
length of the system being measured, not to an arbitrary clock. A
window shorter than one full cycle will catch mid-cycle noise and
mistake it for signal. A window many cycles long will average out
real drift and mistake decay for stability. Somewhere between one and
five cycles tends to be the useful range, though this should be
tuned per deployment rather than assumed.

Third, any metric reported as a single number should be treated with
suspicion until its distribution has been inspected. Two systems can
report identical average accuracy while one is consistently mediocre
and the other alternates between excellent and terrible. These are
not the same system, and a single scalar will never tell you which
one you're looking at.

Fourth, containment failures — moments where the Bounding Layer failed
to keep a glitch inside its defined limits — should be tracked
separately from ordinary performance metrics, and should never be
folded into a single combined health score. A system can be
performing acceptably on average while quietly racking up containment
failures that will eventually compound into a larger breakdown. These
are leading indicators, not lagging ones, and deserve to be watched
as such.

Fifth, and finally: measurement changes the system being measured.
The act of instrumenting a layer adds latency, and latency changes
cycle timing, and cycle timing changes how glitches propagate. There
is no such thing as a perfectly neutral observer inside this
architecture. The goal isn't to eliminate that effect — it can't be
eliminated — but to keep it small, bounded, and documented, the same
way every other irregularity in the system is handled.

Good measurement, in this framework, looks less like a dashboard and
more like a second, slower engine watching the first one — glitched
and bounded in its own right, never claiming a cleaner vantage point
than the thing it's observing.
